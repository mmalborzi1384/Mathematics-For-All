از ابتدای فصل تا اینجا

خلاصهٔ فصل، تا پیش از بخش «موهبت‌های دوسویهٔ بزرگسالان بااستعداد ما»

دعوت از ذهن‌های درخشان برای شکل‌دادن به آیندهٔ ما: آموزش معلمان پیش از خدمت برای دانش‌آموزان بااستعداد ریاضی در آفریقای جنوبی و زیمبابوه

نویسندگان: مایکل کاینوس ملولو و هنری مودندا

۱

چکیده

ریشهٔ آموزش افراد بااستعداد در جنوب آفریقا به دههٔ ۱۸۵۰ و سفر گالتون بازمی‌گردد. نظریه‌های اصلاح نژادی او بر فرضِ فروتری ذهنی آفریقاییان استوار بود. این اندیشه‌ها آموزش افراد بااستعداد را تقریباً به یادگیرندگان سفیدپوست محدود کرد؛ نیازهای یادگیرندگان سیاه‌پوست بااستعداد، حتی پس از بی‌اعتبارشدن اصلاح نژاد، همچنان نادیده ماند. امروز، رقابت در اقتصاد دانش‌بنیان مستلزم پرورش ذهن‌های درخشان است؛ کاری که بدون برنامه‌های آموزشی متمایز و آماده‌سازی معلمان پیش از خدمت ممکن نیست.

این فصل، بزرگسالان بااستعداد را در سه نقشِ «شرور»، «قربانی» و «صلح‌آفرین» بررسی می‌کند. مسیر گالتون و ترمن تا ورورد نشان می‌دهد که چگونه افراد بااستعداد در ساختن نظامی ویژهٔ سفیدپوستان نقش داشتند. در مقابل، تجربهٔ بزرگسالان سیاه‌پوست بااستعداد، هم رنج ناشی از طردشدن را آشکار می‌کند و هم تاب‌آوری آنان را.

کلیدواژه‌ها: آموزش ریاضی · استعداد · نقص‌شناسی · اصلاح نژاد

۲

پیشینه و زمینه

بااستعدادان ریاضی، سرمایهٔ انسانی حیاتی اقتصادهای دانش‌بنیان‌اند. زیمبابوه و آفریقای جنوبی برای دعوت از «صاحبان ذهن‌های درخشان» به شکل‌دادن آیندهٔ کشور، به برنامه‌های ساختاریافتهٔ پرورش استعداد نیاز دارند. به گفتهٔ یونسکو، کیفیت هر نظام آموزشی به کیفیت معلمانش وابسته است. کوکوت (۱۹۹۹) نیز مراکز تربیت معلم را کانون اصلی فعالیت در این حوزه می‌داند.

رین و بیشاپ (۲۰۱۵) بر ضرورت مطالعهٔ بزرگسالان بااستعداد تأکید می‌کنند. گالتون نخستین کسی بود که واژهٔ «بااستعداد» را به کار برد و ترمن پس از او پروژهٔ طولی «مطالعات ژنتیکی نبوغ» را اجرا کرد. هر دو به‌سبب نژادپرستی علمی نقد شده‌اند؛ ۹۵ تا ۹۹ درصد شرکت‌کنندگان پژوهش ترمن سفیدپوست بودند. امروز می‌دانیم استعداد در همهٔ جمعیت‌ها وجود دارد (نگارا، ۲۰۱۷)، اما تصمیم دربارهٔ شناسایی افراد را انسان‌ها می‌گیرند. از همین‌جا دو تجربه شکل می‌گیرد: «نظامی که برای آنان ساخته شده» و «نظامی که برای آنان در نظر گرفته نشده».

۳

بیان مسئله

از زمان گالتون تاکنون، پژوهش‌های اندکی تجربهٔ بزرگسالان بومی آفریقایی را بررسی کرده‌اند؛ افرادی که «نژاد فروتر» انگاشته و از فرصت‌ها کنار گذاشته شدند. نویسندگان تأکید می‌کنند که آفریقاییان برجسته همواره وجود داشته‌اند، اما نژادپرستی علمی سهم آنان را نامرئی کرده است (کیکیانتی و نونگزا، ۲۰۲۵). مستندکردن این سهم، الگوهای نقش فراهم می‌کند و افسانه‌های مربوط به نبود استعداد در جمعیت‌های غیرسفیدپوست را به چالش می‌کشد.

۴

اهداف پژوهش

فصل با الهام از «مثلث درام»، بر پیوستار علت، معلول و راه‌حل بنا شده است. هدف این چارچوب، آگاهی‌بخشیدن به برنامه‌های آماده‌سازی معلمان پیش از خدمت است.

  • شرور؛ علت: بزرگسالان بااستعدادی که نژادپرستی علمی را پیش بردند؛ مانند هندریک ورورد.
  • قربانیان؛ معلول: بزرگسالان بااستعداد به‌حاشیه‌رانده‌شده و موهبت‌های دوسویهٔ موفقیت آنان.
  • صلح‌آفرین؛ راه‌حل: لو ویگوتسکی، سازندهٔ نظریهٔ نقص‌شناسی.
۵

اهمیت پژوهش

بزرگسالان بااستعدادی که از نظامی «ناسازگار با خودشان» عبور کرده‌اند، لزوماً پس از موفقیت ساکت نمی‌شوند. مارک (۲۰۱۷) از «تجی»، دانش‌آموز روستایی بااستعدادی که به دانشگاه ویتواترزرند راه یافت، نقل می‌کند که رسیدن به «سرزمین موعود» پایان مبارزه نیست؛ او خود را «سیاه‌پوست بهتری» نمی‌داند. چنین افرادی با همسالان جامانده همذات‌پنداری می‌کنند و اغلب به مدافعان به‌حاشیه‌رانده‌شدگان بدل می‌شوند.

۶

مهندسی اجتماعی آپارتاید

هر دو کشور تجربهٔ سلطهٔ نژادی داشتند؛ در رودزیا، زیمبابوهٔ امروز، این سلطه از ۱۸۹۵ به‌دست رودز شکل گرفت. هندریک ورورد، دانش‌آموز بااستعداد و استاد روان‌شناسی که بعدها نخست‌وزیر شد، نظام غیررسمی «باسکاپ» را بیش از حد پنهان می‌دانست و نژادپرستی آشکار و قانونی را جایگزین آن کرد؛ ازاین‌رو «معمار آپارتاید» نام گرفت. او در ۱۹۶۰ از یک سوءقصد جان به در برد و در ۱۹۶۶ کشته شد.

ورورد آموزش ریاضی به کودک بانتو را بی‌معنا می‌دانست، زیرا معتقد بود آموزش باید با فرصت‌های زندگی او متناسب باشد. سیاه‌پوستان به بانتوستان‌ها و در رودزیا به «سرزمین‌های امانی قبیله‌ای» رانده شدند. آموزش بانتو سه جهت‌گیری اصلی داشت:

  1. محدودکردن کیفیت تدریس: مدارس مبلغان بسته شد؛ ۹۶٪ معلمان سفیدپوست گواهی داشتند، در برابر فقط ۱۵٪ معلمان سیاه‌پوست.
  2. برنامهٔ درسی برای خدمتکاری: علوم ریاضی برای سیاه‌پوستان غیرضروری شمرده شد.
  3. اولویت‌دادن به آموزش بااستعدادان سفیدپوست: از ترس رقابت دانشمندان و مهندسان سیاه‌پوست.

این میراث همچنان در عملکرد ضعیف دانش‌آموزان سیاه‌پوست در علوم ریاضی دیده می‌شود. جبران آن و پاسخ‌گویی به نیازهای انقلاب صنعتی چهارم، هم ساختارهای مناسب می‌خواهد و هم آماده‌سازی معلمان پیش از خدمت برای آموزش افراد بااستعداد.

۷

گذار به متن ترجمه

پس از حدود ۷۵ سال، نویسندگان به تجربهٔ دو پژوهشگر سیاه‌پوست بااستعداد می‌پردازند: یکی از آفریقای جنوبی، متولد ۱۹۵۳، و دیگری از زیمبابوه، متولد ۱۹۵۵. معیارهای انتخاب عبارت بود از استعداد در علوم ریاضی، پایان‌یافتن دوران کاری، داشتن نمایهٔ مستند، برخاستن از بانتوستان یا سرزمین امانی قبیله‌ای و دفاع کنونی از کودکان بااستعداد. دو نمونهٔ دیگر نیز نام برده شده‌اند: سیابولا زوکسا و مود چیفامبا.

English Text

The Mixed Blessings of Our Gifted Adults

We acknowledge the view that there are so many chance factors that potentially affect the psychological development of gifted students (Cross, 2004). However, the reader needs to recall the three impediments posed by apartheid1 education: (a) gifted education was not for Blacks, hence no identification for them, (b) mathematical sciences was not for Blacks, hence the curriculum was designed accordingly, (c) close all mission schools to limit the training for Black teachers. We argue that it was by chance that our Black gifted scholars went past those three major hurdles. Hence, we foregrounded chance at every stage of our gifted adults' journeys from their places of origin, their families, their high schools, access to good teachers, access to funding and access to good universities.

1. apartheid = South Africa’s former system of racial segregation (آپارتاید؛ نظام پیشین جداسازی و تبعیض نژادی در آفریقای جنوبی)

در آفریقای جنوبی، بحث‌های کنونی درباره‌ی آموزش افراد بااستعداد بدون اشاره به نقش پروفسور لویسو نونگزا ناقص خواهد بود. او ریاضی‌دانی کم‌نظیر و تمام‌عیار از آفریقای جنوبی است و زندگی علمی‌اش به‌خوبی مستند شده است (دووناج، ۲۰۱۹؛ ملولو، ۲۰۲۴؛ کیکیانتی و نونگزا، ۲۰۲۵). افتخارات نونگزا آن‌قدر زیاد است که نمی‌توان همه را برشمرد. از میان آن‌ها، او نخستین فرد از قاره‌ی آفریقا بود که به سمت نایب‌رئیس اجرایی اتحادیه‌ی بین‌المللی ریاضیات۱ انتخاب شد، نخستین سیاه‌پوستی بود که رئیس دانشگاه معتبر ویتواترزرند شد و ریاست بنیاد ملی پژوهش را نیز بر عهده گرفت.

English Text

In South Africa, current debates on gifted education would be incomplete without mentioning the role of Prof. Loyiso Nongxa — a South African mathematician par excellence whose academic profile is well documented (Duvenage, 2019; Mhlolo, 2024; Kikianty & Nongxa, 2025). Nongxa's accolades are too many to list. Still, among them, he became the first person from the African continent to be elected to the executive position of Vice President of the International Mathematical Union1 (IMU), the first black Vice Chancellor of the prestigious University of the Witwatersrand2 (Wits) and Chairperson of the National Research Foundation.

1. IMU = International Mathematical Union (اتحادیه‌ی بین‌المللی ریاضیات)
2. Wits = University of the Witwatersrand (دانشگاه ویتواترزرند)

بسیاری از کسانی که با نونگزا مصاحبه کرده‌اند از او پرسیده‌اند که آیا بااستعداد بوده است، اما او در تمام پاسخ‌هایش دوست ندارد خودش را آدمی خرخوان بداند. این موضوع قابل‌فهم است؛ هم به این دلیل که آموزش افراد بااستعداد هرگز برای سیاه‌پوستان در نظر گرفته نشده بود و بنابراین آن‌ها هیچ‌گاه رسماً بااستعداد شناسایی نشدند، و هم به این دلیل که خود نونگزا نمونه‌ی تواضع است و خودستایی نمی‌کند. این حس تواضع در فرهنگ آفریقایی به‌خوبی شناخته شده است؛ در آن، اوبونتو بر این باور است که اگر به استعداد یا نبوغ خود ببالی، از تو گرفته خواهد شد (نگارا، ۲۰۱۷). با این همه، بسیاری او را «نه یک ریاضی‌دان معمولی»، «راه‌گشا» و «الهام‌بخش بسیاری از جوانان» آفریقای جنوبی توصیف کرده‌اند. برای مثال، پروفسور فان دایک، استاد ریاضیات سال اول نونگزا، از درخشندگی او سخن گفته و اظهار کرده است که در ۳۰ سال تدریسش، نونگزا یکی از بهترین دانشجویانی بوده که داشته، اگر نگوییم بهترین. نونگزا از آغازی فروتنانه در بانتوستان۲ روستایی ترانسکی، در استان کیپ شرقی آفریقای جنوبی، به شهرت رسید؛ جایی که بزرگ شد و روزهایش را به‌عنوان پسرک چوپان گذراند.

English Text

Many who have interviewed Nongxa have asked him whether he was gifted, but in all his responses, he does not like thinking of himself as a nerd. This is understandable, partly because gifted education was never meant for blacks; hence, they were never formally identified as gifted, and partly because Nongxa himself exemplifies humility and does not blow his own trumpet. This sense of humility is well recognised in African culture, where ubuntu1 holds the belief that if you brag about your giftedness or talent, it will be taken away (Ngara, 2017). Yet many have described Nongxa as “not the average mathematician”, “a trailblazer” and an “inspiration to many” young South Africans. For example, Prof. van Dyk, who was Nongxa's first-year mathematics professor, spoke of his star quality, saying that in Dyk's 30 years of teaching, Nongxa was one of the best, if not the best, students he had ever had. Nongxa rose to stardom from humble beginnings in the rural Bantustan2 of Transkei in the Eastern Cape province of South Africa, where he grew up and spent his days as a herd boy.

1. ubuntu = African (Nguni) philosophy of humanity and interconnectedness — “I am because we are” (اوبونتو؛ فلسفه‌ی آفریقایی (نگونی) انسانیت و همبستگی: «من هستم، چون ما هستیم»)
2. Bantustan = territory set aside for Black South Africans under apartheid (بانتوستان؛ سرزمین اختصاص‌یافته به سیاه‌پوستان در دوره‌ی آپارتاید)

در بافت آموزش افراد بااستعداد، او تصادفاً در خانواده‌ای از معلمان به دنیا آمد. تصادفاً از نظر ژنتیکی از استعداد ریاضی برخوردار بود، توانایی ذهنی‌ای که در تمام زندگی‌اش همراهش ماند. تصادفاً به دبیرستان رفت، چون تا سال ۱۹۷۰ تنها ۹/۴ درصد از همه‌ی دانش‌آموزان سیاه‌پوست در مدارس متوسطه بودند، در برابر ۳۶/۴ درصد از سفیدپوستان (تاملین، ۲۰۱۶). تصادفاً درس ریاضی را در دو سال آخر دبیرستان آغاز کرد، مطابق با سیاست زبان بومیِ آموزش بانتو۱ و سیاست برنامه‌ی درسیِ آماده‌سازی برای خدمتکاری. در آن زمان، مدارس مبلغان مذهبی تنها جزایر تعالی‌ای بودند که به دانش‌آموزان سیاه‌پوست آموزشی قابل‌مقایسه با آموزش همتایان سفیدپوستشان ارائه می‌کردند.

English Text

In the context of gifted education, he was born by chance into a family of teachers. It was by chance that he was genetically endowed with mathematical potential — an intellectual prowess that followed him throughout his life. The Gifted Adults' Response to Chance

In their paper on chance and talent development, Gagné and Shader (2006) conducted a comprehensive review of the literature. One problem concerns the subjects' recognition of chance events in their lives. This is an important question, especially given that in some studies chance has been trivialised (Gagné & Shader, 2006). Suppose we take chance as an opportunity that was or could have been missed; in that case, we cannot ignore the question of who created that social arrangement and what their intention was because it has implications for (a) whether and how the victims recognise the chance, (b) the extent to which the victims accept responsibility (such as I only have myself to blame). The question of who must take the blame is key to talentees' emotional judgements of their challenging situations. For example, if students are born gifted by chance to a given family, they cannot attribute the mixed blessings of that chance to the parents, who also have no control over a purely biological outcome.

Vygotsky's Theory as Our Lens

To understand what could be done to improve practice, we needed a theory to guide our argument. In a manner analogous to our argument for understanding gifted education through the experiences of gifted adults, we were also interested in a theory that would explain the experiences of gifted adults who went through an academic system “not meant for them”. In this book chapter, not only do we find an interesting parallel between Vygotsky (a Jew by nationality and religion) and our two profiled gifted adults, but we also find his defectology theory to be more relevant to our arguments for gifted education. In fact, Smagorinsky (2012) argued that Fundamentals of Defectology was not simply a museum piece but one that provides insights into the socialisation of extra-normative children that seem fresh and vital more than a century after their original writing. It is essential to note that the theory has evolved over the past century since its inception in 1924. Therefore, it is neither possible nor the intention of our book chapter to cover every aspect of its tenets, but rather to focus on those relevant to gifted education for discriminated adults. However, to justify its relevance, we first present a brief historical context of its proponent, Lev Vygotsky, and then outline the tenets of the proposed theory.

In a related way, Black Africans were discriminated against during apartheid times. In Imperial Russia, there was antisemitism — a hostility towards or discrimination against Jews as a religious or racial group. It was during the pre-revolutionary period in Russia, when the tsars ruled the country, that Lev Vygotsky was born in November 1896 in Orsha, a small town on the western outskirts of the Russian Empire, now in Belarus. Vygotsky was a precocious student, excelling in his studies from an early age through to university (Ivic, 2024). It was by chance that he was born to a middle-class family, where the father was a bank manager and his mother a teacher. It was by chance that at the Jewish gymnasium1, he was taught by Solomon Ashpiz, a mathematician of note who had spent some time in exile in Siberia for taking part in what were called “revolutionary activities”. There is no doubt that Lev flourished at the gymnasium, as he was a gold medalist. However, Russia introduced a quota system to restrict even the gold-medal-winning Jews, but, by what seemed a miracle, Vygotsky nevertheless won a place at Moscow University through it.

Researchers claim that Vygotsky must have embarked on his new life with a bitter taste in his mouth (Ivic, 2024). This is clear as Vygotsky (2004) asserts that “A creature that is perfectly adapted to its environment would not want anything, would not have anything to strive for, and of course, would not be able to create anything. Thus, creation is always based on lack of adaptation, which gives rise to needs, motives, and desires” (p. 29). This resonates with what Ngara (2017) pointed out: gifted students growing up in a comfort zone have nothing to fight for, whereas those from poverty backgrounds must fight for survival. Jews, including Vygotsky himself, therefore had to use their imaginations to project possibilities that might not have been peculiar to the settled, well-adapted people of the host communities around which Jews had to construct their lives (Kotik-Friedgut & Friedgut, 2008). Hence, at the beginning of his research career at the Psychological Institute in Moscow in 1924, Lev Vygotsky researched defectology and established the Medical-Pedagogic Laboratory for the Study of Abnormal Children in 1926, which was later expanded into the Experimental Institute of Defectology in 1929. The expansion beyond the institute and the discipline has continued in various fields to date.

English Text

Historically, defektologia existed before Lev Vygotsky, but the Russian concept was initially grounded in the belief that children with learning impairments were defective, which might trigger negative connotations. However, both Giest (2018) and Barroco (2018) concur that post-Imperial Russia defectology suggests the opposite of its former meaning. This revolutionary concept of defectology remains relevant in understanding and developing inclusive education systems today. This is because Vygotsky's revolutionised defectology was broader, more positive, optimistic and future-oriented, dedicated to the education of children with differences or exceptional students (Smagorinsky, 2012). In his studies, Vygotsky sought the reasons for handicaps but did not investigate biological ones. Instead, he found that these reasons were not primarily physical “defects” but socially determined handicaps. Therefore, breaking with the common assumption, Vygotsky distinguished between a “primary defect”, or a direct biological impairment, and a “secondary defect”, or the psychological difficulties arising from social arrangements. Using the unfortunate terminology of his time, Vygotsky referred to the first group as defectives (those with primary/biological defects) and the second group as primitives (those with secondary defects), who are not biologically impaired, but whose learning potential is often stunted by the socio-educational environment. Vygotsky's (1993) interest in the secondary disability follows from his conclusion that the effects of difference become social when others view them as stigmas.

Although one may not encounter Vygotsky's explicit use of the term “gifted” in his corpus, upon analysing his concerns with secondary defects arising from social arrangements, it becomes clear how the central tenets of his defectology theory have logical connections to the education of gifted students. For example, suppose one takes the case of gifted Black Africans who were discriminated against based on scientific racism or Jews who were discriminated against based on antisemitism. In that case, one can see typical examples of groups of primitives whose learning potential was stunted by the social arrangements, because being black or being Jewish is not a biological defect. If one considers his labelling terms, one can also see links to gifted students. Vygotsky “never called these children ‘defective’ nor ‘handicapped’ but referred to them as ‘anomalous’” (1993, p.30) — suggesting those students who deviate from the norm. One way of depicting this concept of students who depart from the evolutionary norm or children of difference has been through a bell curve featuring the bulk of the learners in the middle (the norm), and two groups that deviate from the norm (anomalous) made up of learners with learning difficulties on the left-hand side and the advanced learners (gifted) on the right-hand side.

While Vygotsky's revolutionised defectology points to the need to meet the needs of the two groups who are anomalous, in critiquing efforts provided by the special education approaches of his day, Vygotsky's (1993) major concern was with the customary approach of attempting to amplify the underdeveloped or absent capacity (those below average on the left-hand side) towards the norm. With such an approach, he believed that few children, much less those with evolutionary differences, reach their full potential; children within the evolutionary norm rarely reach the upper limits of their possibilities, while extranormal children are often stuck at the lowest end of their social potential (Smagorinsky, 2012). Vygotsky (1993) described this as an “…invalid-oriented point of view toward difference” (p. 75), hence defective or narrow. Instead, he conceptualised a revolutionary approach as one that recognises untapped potential and provides enriching environments for all learners, including those below average, average and gifted (Barroco, 2018; Vik & Somby, 2018).

In terms of providing enriching environments, Vygotsky (1995) was concerned about standardised curriculum and instruction that promote reproduction rather than production of something new. In Vygotsky's view, it was not important to know just what already exists in children's minds because people will just reproduce activities that do not lead to something new and will thus be linked to memory (Ness, 2020). Vygotsky (1978) defined the zona blizhayshego razvitiya, or Zone of Proximal Development1 (ZPD), as “the distance between the actual developmental level as determined by independent problem solving and the level of potential development as determined through problem solving under adult guidance or in collaboration with more capable peers” (p. 86). Although Vygotsky introduced the concept, there is no evidence that he himself produced a visual diagram of the ZPD. The familiar concentric-circle representations and associated terminology were developed later by other scholars to render the theoretical structure more explicit. Building on this tradition, Taber (2025) recently elaborated the ZPD as a form of “phase space” spanning learners' competencies before and after instruction. This conceptualisation begins with the learner's current level of independent functioning, commonly referred to as the Zone of Actual (or Achieved) Development (ZAD2). The term proximal, meaning “near”, denotes those skills and understandings that are in the process of maturation that learners cannot yet perform independently but can achieve with guidance and collaboration from a More Knowledgeable Other3 (MKO), such as a teacher, parent or peer.

Nardo (2021) further emphasised that the ZPD1 should be understood not as a fixed cognitive gap but as a dialogical space of potential that emerges from the interaction between the learner's present capabilities (shaped by both biological endowment and historical experience), the sociocultural context and the educator's capacity to recognise and scaffold emerging development. Admittedly, ZPD is difficult to measure, but Vygotsky's (1993) vision for the education of exceptional learners was geared towards the ultimate developmental goal of achieving social status. Vygotsky (1978) recognised that the distance between performing a task independently and with the help of another person indicated stages of development that do not necessarily coincide across individuals. Hence, within the revolutionised defectology, the view of education is that it should enable students to reach their individual potential and become meaningful contributors to society (Smagorinsky, 2012). Once education is understood as the process of enabling individuals to reach their maximum potential, the close connection between Vygotsky's theory of defectology and his concept of the zona blizhayshego razvitiya — the zone of proximal development (ZPD) becomes evident.

English Text

The ZPD1 has since become a key concept within gifted education. As Taber (2025) notes, although definitions of giftedness vary, in any educational context, there will be students who are significantly more advanced than their peers in mathematics. Importantly, as Parish (2016) argues, this does not imply that other students are incapable of achieving similar levels of success; rather, it reflects the reality that students within the same grade operate within different zones of proximal development.

Viewed through this lens, Vygotsky's critique of standardised curricula and assessment practices can be seen as a central feature of his defectology: such practices often constrain gifted learners by focusing instruction within their zone of actual development (ZAD) rather than fostering progression towards their ZPD. Beyond the ZPD lies a further zone variously described as the “zone of frustration” or “beyond reach” — which represents learning that is unattainable even with expert guidance or the support of a more knowledgeable other3 (MKO). While some interpretations of learning suggest that “everything is possible” if boundaries are pushed, Vygotsky's framework makes clear that these boundaries are inherently individual and cannot be uniform without denying human diversity. Human capacity is finite, shaped by physical, psychological and intellectual constraints. Empirical research supports this position, demonstrating that some learners will not achieve success in particular domains, such as mathematics, even with effort and appropriate support, if they lack the requisite capacity or natural potential (Gagné, 2019). It is within this context that Taber (2025) introduces the concept of the “zone of no development4” (ZND).

Implications for the Teacher

The teacher's role in Vygotsky's theory of defectology is fundamental. In this closing section of our book chapter, there are two angles from which one can see the implications for that role. Firstly, from a theoretical perspective, we acknowledge that some researchers argue that Vygotsky's writing on defectology is short on practical examples of the education he envisioned. However, if one follows Vygotsky's view about anomalous children, he believed that his futuristic schools of defectology needed to be spoken about as schools of exceptional learning whose goal it was to promote, with attention to developmental issues, integration of extranormal children into the current of social life, such that upon their graduation they exhibited the higher mental functions that enabled full participation in this full social stream (Vygotsky, 2004). Therefore, if educational experts considered it necessary to train special teachers to integrate exceptional children into the current social life, it can be argued that traditional practices have erred by focusing on deficits, training special teachers only for people with disabilities, while neglecting the gifted children who are also anomalous. According to Vygotsky's futuristic view, this means there should be a teacher who understands and can therefore meet the needs of children who are below average, average and above average. This would enable children in each of those levels to develop to their full potential or ZPD1. Let us recall that, in Vygotsky's view, the teacher, as an expert or MKO2, is the key to the ZPD, which he defined as the range of skills students can achieve with guidance and collaboration from an expert. Similar studies have confirmed that the teacher's mediation is essential to intervene in the ZPD of gifted students, thereby developing their potential and talents (Piske et al., 2017).

1. ZPD = Zone of Proximal Development (منطقه‌ی رشد مجاور)
2. MKO = More Knowledgeable Other (دیگریِ آگاه‌تر)

English Text

Conclusion

This position paper was prompted by growing concern that both Zimbabwe and South Africa are under increasing pressure to convert student potential into the skills required for participation in the twenty-first-century knowledge economy. While such transformation necessitates the establishment of coherent and well-structured talent development programmes, gifted education in both contexts has largely failed to gain traction. A key reason for this failure is the absence of systematic preparation for teaching gifted learners within higher education institutions, which continue to neglect the training of pre-service teachers for gifted education. In response, this position paper is structured along a cause–effect–solution continuum to trace the roots of this neglect and propose theoretically grounded interventions. Our central argument is that the erosion of gifted education in both Zimbabwe and South Africa cannot be understood independently of the historical legacy of scientific racism, most notably institutionalised under the intellectual and political influence of Hendrik Verwoerd, himself a gifted adult. This legacy has had enduring consequences for how ability, intelligence and potential are conceptualised and distributed within education systems. Through the contrasting profiles of two gifted adults, the chapter illustrates both the transformative possibilities that emerge when giftedness is recognised and nurtured, and the profound waste of human potential that results when it is systematically ignored or suppressed. These profiles serve to underscore the long-term societal costs of failing to develop gifted learners, particularly in contexts seeking scientific and technological advancement.

English Text

Vygotsky's theory of defectology provides both a conceptual justification and a practical framework for addressing this challenge. It not only explains why gifted learners should be enabled to reach their full developmental potential but also offers guidance on how teachers can be prepared to do so. Central to this framework is the need for teacher education programmes to equip educators with the knowledge and skills required to design curricula and pedagogical strategies that intentionally target learners' zones of proximal development (ZPD1). In contrast to deficit-oriented approaches, Vygotsky's defectology emphasises development, mediation and the role of instruction in extending learners beyond their current level of functioning. In alignment with this revolutionary perspective, the chapter concludes by recommending that post-democratic governments in both Zimbabwe and South Africa prioritise the formal training of specialist teachers for gifted education. Without such targeted investment, efforts to cultivate globally competitive scientists and innovators are unlikely to succeed. Recognising and developing giftedness, particularly through theoretically informed teacher preparation, is therefore not a luxury but a strategic necessity for national development in the contemporary global landscape.

دانشگاه را پس از حدود نُه سال بی‌ثباتی در رهبری، با تراز مالی پایدار تحویل داد: نظام‌های مالی را بازسازی کرد، برنامه‌های درسی را بازنگری کرد و برنامه‌ی «تحول و برابری شغلی» را با پروژه‌ای حدود ۲ میلیارد رند پیش برد؛ «ویت نو» عبارتی بود که برای بیان چشم‌اندازش به کار می‌برد. از ۲۰۱۴ تا ۲۰۱۸ مدیر بنیان‌گذار مرکز علوم ریاضی و محاسباتی ویتس و هم‌زمان رئیس هیئت مدیره‌ی بنیاد ملی پژوهش (NRF) بود؛ در ۲۰۱۸ به‌عنوان استاد ریاضیات بازنشسته شد و در همان سال در مجمع عمومی اتحادیه‌ی بین‌المللی ریاضی (IMU) در سائوپائولو به یکی از دو سمت نایب‌رئیسی برگزیده شد. سال‌های ۲۰۱۹ تا ۲۰۲۰ هم مدیر ویژه‌ی دانشگاه فورت هر بود.

مایکل کاینوس مهلولو

Michael Kainose Mhlolo

پژوهشگر آموزش ریاضی و استعداد ریاضی · زاده‌ی ۱۹۵۵ در رودزیا

پرونده۰۲ از ۰۳

مایکل کاینوس مهلولو در ۱۹۵۵ در موندورو (Mhondoro) به دنیا آمد؛ یکی از «سرزمین‌های امانی قبیله‌ای» (TTL) در ناحیه‌ی کادوما، استان ماشونالند غربی، در رودزیای آن زمان. کودکی‌اش را همان‌جا گذراند و بخشی از جوانی‌اش را به‌عنوان پسرک چوپان.

نامش را کاینوس (Kainos) گذاشتند؛ واژه‌ای یونانی به معنای «یگانه»، «بی‌سابقه»، «نو» یا «کمیاب». بعدها مایکل نامیده شد و خودش به شوخی به «جبر نامی» اشاره می‌کند: اینکه آدم‌ها به‌سمت کاری کشیده می‌شوند که نامشان به آن اشاره دارد.

در کالج کوتاما (Kutama College) تحصیل کرد، دبیرستان کاتولیک معتبری که به سیاه‌پوستان آفریقایی آموزش عالی می‌داد و رابرت موگابه و برنارد چیدزرو هم از فارغ‌التحصیلانش بودند. مدرک کارشناسی ارشد را از دانشگاه زیمبابوه گرفت و سپس با بورسیه‌ی وزارت توسعه‌ی بین‌المللی بریتانیا (DfID) دکتری آموزش ریاضیات را در دانشگاه ویتواترزرند خواند. با این حال، می‌گوید هرگز خود را «بااستعداد» نمی‌داند.

عضو شورای جهانی کودکان بااستعداد و مستعد (WGCTC) است و در تدوین «۱۰ اصل جهانی» آن در ۲۰۲۱ مشارکت داشت؛ سه سال عضو هیئت اجرایی گروه بین‌المللی خلاقیت و استعداد ریاضی (MCG)، عضو کمیسیون ICME و انجمن بین‌المللی پژوهش هوش (ISIR). رتبه‌ی C پژوهش در حوزه‌ی استعداد ریاضی و کمک‌هزینه‌ی بنیاد ملی پژوهش (NRF) را هم دارد؛ کمک‌هزینه‌ای که امکان داد دانشجویان تحصیلات تکمیلی زیمبابوه و آفریقای جنوبی را در آموزش افراد بااستعداد راهنمایی کند.

در فصل کتاب، این ارقام را به سه مانع گره می‌زند: دسترسی‌نداشتن دانش‌آموزان بااستعداد به پروفایل حرفه‌ای و شبکه‌ی بورس‌ها، بزرگ‌شدن در بانتوسان‌ها و «سرزمین‌های امانی» فقیر، و نبود نظام شناسایی استعداد. راه‌حل او تربیت معلم، بازنگری برنامه‌ی درسی، ساختن مدارس ویژه و جایگزینی «تصادف» با شناسایی نظام‌مند است.

Lev Semyonovich Vygotsky

لو سمنوویچ ویگوتسکی در ۱۷ نوامبر ۱۸۹۶ (۵ نوامبر به تقویم قدیم) در شهر اورشا (Orsha) در امپراتوری روسیه — امروز در بلاروس — به دنیا آمد؛ در خانواده‌ای یهودی، غیرمذهبی و متمول. پدرش، سمیون لوویچ، بانکدار بود و مادرش، سیسیلیا موسیونا، معلم. یک سال پس از تولدش، خانواده به شهر گومل (Gomel) رفت و او تا ۱۹۱۱ در خانه آموزش دید و سپس با درجه‌ی ممتاز از یک ژیمنازیوم خصوصی یهودی فارغ‌التحصیل شد.

ریاضیات را نزد سولومون آشپیز خواند؛ ریاضی‌دانی که به‌خاطر شرکت در آنچه «فعالیت‌های انقلابی» خوانده می‌شد مدتی در تبعید سیبری بود. در ۱۹۱۳ سهمیه‌ی سه‌درصدی ورود یهودیان به دانشگاه‌های مسکو اعمال شد — «قرعه»ای که راه را می‌بست. برخلاف میل پدر، ابتدا پزشکی را انتخاب کرد و در همان سال در رشته‌ی حقوق ثبت‌نام کرد و هم‌زمان در رشته‌های علوم انسانی و علوم اجتماعی دانشگاه شانیافسکی شرکت می‌کرد. پس از انقلاب اکتبر ۱۹۱۷ تحصیلات رسمی را رها کرد و به گومل بازگشت.
